Straight Answers About Your Teeth

Here is a simple way to think about dental care. Your teeth need a check twice a year, a cleaning to clear tartar, and a fix when something breaks or hurts. If your gums bleed, that is a sign to act, not a sign to brush harder and hope it stops. If a tooth is sensitive to cold, it may have a crack, a worn filling, or a root that is showing. If a tooth aches at night, it may need more than a filling. The team will look, take an X-ray if needed, and tell you what they find. They will also tell you what can wait, what should be fixed soon, and what each choice may cost. You do not have to decide on the spot. Take the plan home, talk it over, and call back with questions. That is how good dental care should work, and it is how this office tries to work with every patient.

Emergency

What counts as a dental emergency? Pain that will not stop, swelling, a tooth that has been knocked out, or a tooth that has broken in a way that leaves a sharp edge. Those need a call the same day. Call (561) 260-5575 and tell the team what happened. They will try to fit you in soon, and the first step is to ease the pain. If a tooth is knocked out, keep it damp and bring it with you. If a crown comes off, keep it safe and do not try to glue it back on your own. Rinse with warm water, and do not chew on the sore side. A lost filling or a cracked tooth may not hurt much at first, but it still needs a look, because food and germs can get into the crack. After the visit, the team will set up any follow-up work, so the same tooth does not cause trouble again.

Invisalign®

Do clear trays really work? Yes, for many people they do. Invisalign uses a series of trays that move teeth a little at a time. You change sets every week or two, and you take the trays out to eat and brush. They are hard to see, which is why many adults and teens choose them over metal braces. They can help with crowded teeth, gaps, and bites that do not line up. The catch is that you need to wear them most of the day, and you need to keep them clean. If you do not, the teeth will not move as planned. Dr. Weiss will check your teeth and bite first, then tell you if trays are a good choice, how long they may take, and what they may cost for your mouth. He will see you at set points to check how the teeth are moving.

General Dentistry

What happens at a routine checkup? The team cleans your teeth, checks for decay, and looks at your gums for swelling or bleeding. They take X-rays when they are due, and they check your bite, your jaw, and any old fillings or crowns. A small cavity can be filled in one visit, before it turns into a toothache. Gum care is just as important, because sore or bleeding gums can lead to bone loss around teeth if they are left alone. If you grind your teeth at night, Dr. Weiss can talk about a night guard. If a tooth feels sharp or rough, he can smooth it or repair it. Most people do best with a checkup and cleaning twice a year. Bring a list of medicines, and tell the team about any change in your health, so they can plan your care safely.

Cosmetic Dentistry

How can you change the way your smile looks without changing who you are? Start small. Whitening can lift stains from coffee, tea, and time. Bonding can fix a chip or a worn edge in one visit, with a material that matches your tooth. Veneers can change the shape and shade of front teeth that are stained, uneven, or a little short. Dr. Weiss talks with you about what you like and what you would change, then suggests the smallest step that will make a real difference. A good result looks natural, not like a different person. If you have a wedding, a new job, or a big photo coming up, tell the team early, so they can plan the timing with you.

Oral Surgery

When does a tooth need to come out? When it is badly broken, when it has a deep infection that cannot be fixed, or when it is too crowded to keep. Wisdom teeth are a common case, when they have no room to come in straight or they keep causing pain. Dr. Weiss has years of training in this work, and he explains what will happen before he starts. He looks at X-rays, talks about your health and your medicines, and tells you what to expect. After surgery, you get simple steps for rest, food, and pain relief, plus a number to call if you have a worry. Most people heal best when they follow those steps, eat soft foods for a short time, and keep the area clean. Once you have healed, the team talks about replacing the tooth, so your bite stays even.

Dental Implants

Is an implant worth it? For many people, yes, because it replaces a missing tooth with a fixed one. A small post is placed in the jaw, and over time it joins with the bone. A crown is then fitted on top, shaped and shaded to match your other teeth. Implants do not lean on the teeth next door, and they do not slip when you eat or talk. They can replace one tooth, or help hold a bridge or a denture in place. The work takes more than one visit, and healing takes time, so the team will give you a clear time frame. Dr. Weiss checks your bone, your gums, and your health first. If an implant is not right for you, he will talk about a bridge or a partial denture, and help you weigh the choices.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a checkup if nothing hurts?

Yes. Many problems, such as small cavities and early gum trouble, do not hurt at first. A checkup and cleaning twice a year can catch them early, when the fix is small and the cost is lower.

How much will my visit cost?

It depends on what you need. A checkup and cleaning cost less than a crown or an implant. The team can help you check your insurance before you book, and they will talk through the cost of a plan before you decide.

What should I bring to my first visit?

Bring a list of medicines, a note about any pain or worry, and your insurance card if you have one. The team will check your teeth and gums, take X-rays if needed, and talk through what they find in plain words.

Do you see new patients?

Yes, new patients are welcome. Call (561) 260-5575 or book through the practice website. The office does not accept Affinity, Fidelis, Health First, or Medicaid, but it works with many other plans.

Can a missing tooth be fixed?

Yes. An implant, a bridge, or a partial denture can fill the gap. Dr. Weiss will check your bone, your gums, and your health, then talk about which choice fits you best.

Are clear trays a good idea for me?

They can be, if your teeth are crowded, gapped, or a little out of line. A consult can tell you if trays will work, how long they may take, and what they may cost. You need to wear them most of the day for them to work well.

I feel nervous. Can you help me through it?

Yes. Tell the team when you book, so they can allow extra time and go slowly. You can ask for breaks, and you can bring music or a friend. A calm first visit often makes the next one much easier.
